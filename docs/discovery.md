Aurora Jewels — Product Discovery Analysis
1. Business
Confirmed requirements
Aurora Jewels is a premium jewelry e-commerce brand.
Products are elegant, modern, luxurious, and intended to remain wearable for everyday use.
Initial market focus is Pakistan.
The brand should feel sophisticated, timeless, exclusive, and premium.
The website should differentiate Aurora Jewels from generic/cluttered jewelry stores.
Assumptions
The primary commercial channel will be the website.
The brand is currently growing and expects its product catalog and customer base to expand substantially.
Questions
What is Aurora Jewels' exact current product range?
What jewelry materials/qualities are offered?
Is the business currently operating, launching from scratch, or redesigning an existing store?
Does Aurora Jewels have a physical location today?
Risks
A premium positioning can be undermined by inconsistent photography, copy, checkout UX, packaging expectations, or customer service.
A 4–6 week timeline may become difficult if branding, content, product data, integrations, and payment/shipping setup are not ready early.

Key Risks
Scope risk: Branding + e-commerce + custom admin + motion + integrations + content creation is substantial for a 4–6 week launch.
Content risk: 50–100 products need complete, accurate commercial content and imagery.
Integration risk: Payment and courier providers have not been selected.
Performance risk: Luxury imagery and sophisticated motion can conflict with the requirement for speed.
UX risk: A highly editorial experience could make product discovery or checkout less efficient if not carefully balanced.
Operational risk: COD introduces fulfillment and return-related complexity.
Branding risk: The visual identity is still open, so design direction could cause iteration.
SEO risk: Target keywords may be competitive, and organic results will take time.
Security/compliance risk: E-commerce security expectations have not been defined.
Timeline risk: The 4–6 week target depends heavily on fast client decisions, complete content, product data, payment/courier onboarding, and policy readiness.
Product Discovery Conclusion

The brief establishes a premium, Pakistan-first jewelry e-commerce experience with strong brand storytelling and sophisticated but restrained motion. The core business requirements are reasonably clear; the biggest discovery gaps are budget, commerce operations, payment/shipping providers, product/catalog data, brand/content ownership, authentication details, analytics expectations, accessibility, security, and measurable performance targets.

The most important next step is requirements clarification and scope prioritization, not technical architecture. In particular, the team should distinguish launch-critical functionality from future capabilities such as international commerce, Journal expansion, abandoned-cart marketing, advanced customer marketing, Urdu, and 3D.
