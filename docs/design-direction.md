Aurora Jewels — Creative Direction

Direction classification: Luxury + Editorial + Minimal Clean + Soft + Light + Immersive
Secondary: Bold typography, restrained motion, sophisticated digital commerce.

The visual system should position Aurora Jewels as a premium jewelry house, not a conventional e-commerce store. The experience should feel editorial and exclusive while keeping product discovery, cart, and checkout highly intuitive. This follows the confirmed requirement for luxury presentation, generous whitespace, sophisticated typography, deep emerald/teal, champagne/gold, ivory and black, with restrained motion.

1. Visual Personality

Sophisticated, timeless, feminine, intimate, refined and quietly luxurious.

Avoid the typical jewelry-store aesthetic of:

Excessive gold
Ornamental borders
Heavy gradients
Glitter effects
Overly decorative typography
Dense product grids
Excessive animation

The luxury should come from composition, photography, typography, spacing and restraint, rather than visual ornamentation.

2. Color Psychology
Deep Emerald/Teal: exclusivity, sophistication, richness and distinction.
Champagne/Gold: luxury, preciousness and craftsmanship; use as an accent rather than dominant color.
Ivory: warmth, elegance and premium editorial space.
Black: authority, contrast and sophistication.
White/soft neutrals: clarity and breathing room.

The source specifically confirms deep emerald/teal, champagne/gold, ivory and black as the intended visual family.

Recommended Palette
Role	Direction
Primary	Deep Emerald / sophisticated teal
Secondary	Warm Ivory
Accent	Muted Champagne Gold
Strong Contrast	Near Black
Surface	Ivory / soft warm white
Text	Deep charcoal / black
Muted text	Warm gray

Gold should be an accent, not the primary interface color.

3. Typography Direction

Use a high-contrast luxury editorial serif for major brand statements, collection titles and hero messaging, paired with a clean modern sans-serif for navigation, product information, pricing, forms and checkout.

Typography should create the feeling of a fashion/editorial publication while maintaining excellent commerce usability.

Recommended hierarchy:

Display: elegant serif, large and spacious
Headings: refined serif
Body/UI: contemporary sans-serif
Labels: small uppercase sans-serif with generous letter spacing
Price: clean, highly legible sans-serif

Avoid overly ornamental script fonts.

4. Layout Personality

Editorial first, commerce second — but never at the expense of conversion.

Use:

Large photographic compositions
Generous whitespace
Asymmetrical editorial sections
Strong vertical rhythm
Large typography
Full-width storytelling moments
Carefully controlled product grids
Clear visual hierarchy

The homepage should feel closer to a luxury fashion/jewelry editorial than a generic Shopify-style storefront.

Product listing and checkout areas should become more structured and efficient.

5. Imagery Style

Photography is one of the primary luxury signals.

Use:

High-resolution jewelry photography
Clean backgrounds
Macro/detail shots
Elegant lifestyle photography
Soft natural or studio lighting
Close-up craftsmanship details
Editorial compositions
Consistent crop ratios

Product imagery should remain the visual hero on product pages.

The discovery confirms existing product photography but notes that consistency and final photography readiness are important launch dependencies.

6. Icon Style

Use minimal line icons with:

Thin strokes
Simple geometry
No filled decorative icons
Consistent stroke weight
Subtle animation where appropriate

Examples:

Search
Account
Wishlist
Bag
Menu
Arrow
Plus/minus
Close

Icons should feel like part of a luxury editorial system rather than a generic UI icon pack.

7. Card Style

Product cards should be quiet and image-led.

Preferred:

Minimal or no visible card container
Large image area
Generous spacing
Product name below image
Price clearly visible
Small understated labels
Wishlist control appearing subtly

Avoid:

Heavy shadows
Thick borders
Excessive badges
Rounded "app-like" cards
Dense metadata

The product itself should provide the visual richness.

8. Button Style

Buttons should be confident but understated.

Primary CTA:

Dark emerald/black background
Ivory/light text
Moderate rectangular proportions
Slightly softened corners
Strong typography
Subtle hover transition

Secondary CTA:

Transparent
Fine border
Dark text
Minimal hover treatment

Avoid pill-shaped buttons throughout the site; they would push the brand toward a contemporary SaaS aesthetic rather than luxury fashion.

9. Border / Radius Style

Use low-radius or near-square geometry.

Recommended:

0–4px radius for major commerce components
Minimal borders
1px subtle dividers
Fine champagne/gold lines only where they genuinely enhance hierarchy

The system should feel architectural and editorial rather than bubbly or playful.

10. Shadow Style

Use shadows sparingly.

Preferred:

Very soft
Large blur
Low opacity
Used primarily to establish layering

Do not rely on strong card shadows.

Luxury should come from contrast and spacing, not elevation effects.

11. Motion Intensity

Overall intensity: Low–Medium.

Motion is explicitly confirmed, including smooth transitions, product reveals, subtle scroll animations, premium hover effects and micro-interactions.

Motion hierarchy:

Marketing/homepage: Medium
Collection/product browsing: Low–Medium
Product detail: Medium
Cart: Low
Checkout: Very Low
Admin: Minimal

The shopping journey should always feel faster than the marketing experience.

12. 2D Animation Style

Use sophisticated 2D motion such as:

Image reveal masks
Gentle image scale on hover
Text fade/slide reveals
Editorial section transitions
Underline animations
Product-image transitions
Soft opacity changes
Subtle parallax
Page-to-page transition continuity
Staggered content reveals

Animation should feel silky and intentional, never flashy.

Avoid:

Bounce
Elastic effects
Excessive rotation
Constant floating objects
Large UI movement
Animation on every element
13. Mouse Interaction Style

Desktop interactions should be subtle and tactile.

Examples:

Product image gently scales on hover
Wishlist icon fades/reveals
CTA underline expands
Images transition smoothly between states
Cursor interaction can subtly emphasize premium editorial elements

A custom cursor is not recommended as a core requirement. It can easily become decorative friction.

14. Scroll Interaction Style

Use scroll as a storytelling mechanism.

Recommended:

Slow image reveals
Text entering viewport progressively
Subtle parallax
Editorial image transitions
Sticky collection/product navigation where useful
Gentle section-to-section continuity

Do not make scrolling dependent on animation.

A user should still be able to rapidly browse products.

15. 3D Usage

Recommendation: Do not make 3D part of the core launch experience.

The approved requirements explicitly classify 3D as optional and require speed and mobile-friendliness. Full 3D experiences, modeling and asset production are not confirmed.

For Aurora Jewels, sophisticated 2D motion + exceptional photography will provide a stronger luxury result with less performance and implementation risk.

Potential future 3D use could be considered only if there is a specific product experience where interactive viewing demonstrably improves customer confidence or understanding.

16. 3D Intensity

Launch intensity: 0 / none.

Future possibility: Very Low.

Do not introduce 3D simply to make the site look technologically impressive.

17. Mobile Adaptation

Mobile should not be treated as a compressed desktop version.

Priorities:

Large but efficiently loaded product imagery
Clear hierarchy
Simple navigation
Thumb-friendly controls
Fast product browsing
Minimal animation
Sticky or easily accessible purchase controls where appropriate
Simplified editorial compositions
Reduced parallax
No unnecessary decorative interactions

The SRS explicitly requires responsive/mobile-friendly behavior and emphasizes that motion and imagery must not compromise performance.

Mobile visual principle:

Preserve the luxury, remove the friction.

18. Accessibility Considerations

No specific accessibility standard is currently confirmed, so no WCAG level should be presented as a contractual requirement.

Nevertheless, the design system should proactively support:

Strong text/background contrast
Visible keyboard focus states
Keyboard-accessible navigation
Accessible form controls
Semantic heading hierarchy
Descriptive image alt text
Clear error states
Touch targets appropriate for mobile
No information communicated by color alone
Motion that can be reduced/disabled where appropriate
No essential interaction dependent solely on hover
Final Art Direction
Brand Feel

"Quiet luxury with editorial confidence."

Aurora Jewels should feel like a modern luxury jewelry editorial that happens to be an exceptionally easy-to-use store.

Visual Formula

Ivory space + deep emerald/black + restrained champagne accents + sophisticated serif typography + contemporary sans-serif UI + exceptional jewelry photography + generous whitespace + subtle motion.
